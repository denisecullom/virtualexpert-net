How do virtual assistants use AI to handle a client's customer support?

Customer support is one of the easiest AI services for a VA to sell: the questions repeat, the answers already exist in old emails, and every slow reply costs the client a customer. The trick is keeping a person in charge of what gets sent.

By Denise Cullom · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

Start by building an FAQ from the client's last few months of support emails: the common questions, the approved answers and the policies behind them. Each morning, have AI sort the queue into routine, needs-a-decision and urgent. For routine messages, AI drafts a reply from the FAQ in the client's voice and you review it before it goes out. Complaints, refunds outside policy, legal or safety issues and anything emotional always go to a person. Once a month, send a feedback report: the top questions, recurring problems and the FAQ changes they suggest. Charge a setup fee for the FAQ and a monthly fee for running it.

Most small businesses answer the same twenty questions over and over: where's my order, how do I change my booking, do you ship to Canada, can I get a refund. The owner either answers them late at night or lets them pile up. A VA with a good AI setup can clear that queue every morning, in the owner's voice, with far less effort than answering each one from scratch.

What makes it work isn't the AI writing. It's the FAQ behind it, and the rules about what a person always handles.

How do you build a support FAQ with AI?

  1. Collect the history. Export three to six months of support emails or messages. Remove card numbers and other sensitive details first.
  2. Group the questions. Ask AI to sort them into topics and count how often each one comes up.
  3. Draft the answers. For each common question, take the best reply the client actually sent and turn it into a standard answer.
  4. Check the policies. Go through refunds, returns, delivery times and pricing with the client. The FAQ must match their real rules, not what the AI assumes.
  5. Get it approved. The client signs off the FAQ. From then on, it's the source for every routine reply.

The FAQ is valuable on its own. Many clients put it on their website, which cuts the number of questions before you've answered a single one.

How does the morning queue sort work?

Each morning, AI reads the new messages and puts each one in a group:

  • Routine: the FAQ answers it. AI drafts the reply.
  • Needs a decision: an exception, a special request, or something the FAQ doesn't cover. You or the client decides.
  • Urgent: an angry customer, a delivery that's gone wrong, anything that gets worse with time. Handled first.

You check the sort before acting on it. It takes a few minutes and catches the odd message in the wrong pile.

How do you keep AI replies sounding human?

Give the AI the client's voice: their usual greeting and sign-off, a few real replies they're proud of, and words to avoid. Ask for short replies that answer the question in the first line. Then review every draft before it's sent, at least for the first month. Fix anything that sounds stiff, and add the fix to the voice notes so the next draft is better. The approach in using AI without sounding like AI applies here too.

What should always go to a person?

  • Complaints and anyone who's upset.
  • Refunds or exceptions outside the written policy.
  • Anything about safety, health, legal threats or data requests.
  • Press, partners and large customers.
  • Anything where the AI isn't sure, which you should ask it to flag rather than guess.

Write these rules into the setup and into your agreement with the client. Being clear about them is what makes clients comfortable handing support over.

What goes in the monthly feedback report?

Support emails are the client's best market research, and almost nobody reads them that way. Once a month, send one page: the top five questions and how many times each came up, recurring problems (late deliveries, a confusing checkout step), what customers praised, and suggested FAQ or website changes. Add the hours saved, measured against the baseline, as in the monthly results report.

What should a VA charge for AI customer support?

Charge a setup fee for building the FAQ, the voice notes and the sort, and a monthly fee for running and improving it. The Customer Support Kit suggests a setup fee of $600 to $1,200, and the pricing guide shows how to work out the monthly fee from the time saved.

Which prompts should you use?

The first builds the FAQ from past emails:

1

Build the FAQ from past emails

Prompt

Here are my client's support emails from the last [months], with personal details removed: [paste]. Step 1: Group the customer questions into topics and count how often each comes up. Step 2: For the 15 most common, write a standard answer based on the best reply the business actually sent. Step 3: Mark any answer that depends on a policy (refunds, delivery times, pricing) with [check with client]. Step 4: List the questions that came up but had no good answer. Use only what's in the emails.

The second sorts the morning queue and drafts the routine replies:

2

Sort the queue and draft replies

Prompt

You handle the morning support queue for [business]. Use the approved FAQ and voice notes in this project. For each new message: Step 1: Label it routine, needs a decision, or urgent. Anything upset, outside policy, legal, safety-related or unclear is never routine. Step 2: For routine messages, draft a reply under 120 words that answers the question in the first line, in the client's voice. Step 3: For the others, write one line on what's needed and from whom. Step 4: Give me the urgent ones first. Don't send anything.

The third writes the monthly feedback report:

3

Write the monthly feedback report

Prompt

Here are this month's support messages for [business], with personal details removed: [paste]. Write a one-page report for the owner: the top five questions with counts, recurring problems and how often they came up, what customers praised, and three suggested changes to the FAQ or website. Plain English, no jargon. Only include what's in the messages.

Start with the FAQ. Even before you touch the queue, a client who sees their twenty most common questions answered on one page understands exactly what you're offering.

Frequently asked questions

Can a virtual assistant use AI for customer support?

Yes. A VA builds an FAQ from the client's past support emails, has AI sort the queue each morning and draft routine replies in the client's voice, reviews every draft before it's sent, and passes complaints, exceptions and sensitive issues to a person.

How do you build a customer support FAQ with AI?

Export three to six months of support emails, remove sensitive details, ask AI to group and count the questions, turn the best real replies into standard answers, check every policy with the client and get the FAQ approved.

Which customer messages should AI never answer on its own?

Complaints, upset customers, refunds or exceptions outside policy, safety, health, legal and data requests, press and large customers, and anything the AI isn't sure about.

How do you stop AI support replies sounding robotic?

Give the AI the client's greeting, sign-off, real example replies and words to avoid, ask for short replies that answer in the first line, and review every draft before it's sent, adding your fixes to the voice notes.

What should a VA charge for AI customer support?

A setup fee for building the FAQ, voice notes and queue sort, often $600 to $1,200, plus a monthly fee for running and improving it, based on the time it saves the client.
