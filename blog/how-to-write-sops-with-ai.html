How do virtual assistants use AI to write SOPs for clients?

Every business runs on processes that live in someone's head. Writing them down used to take hours nobody had. With AI, a VA can turn a ten-minute walkthrough into a clear SOP in under half an hour, and clients will pay for it.

By Denise Cullom · November 6, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Don't write SOPs from scratch. Record the task being done once, either a screen recording with you talking through it or a voice note, and get a transcript. Give AI the transcript and a fixed format, and it drafts the SOP: purpose, when it runs, what you need, numbered steps, decisions, checks, and what to do when it goes wrong. You fix what it got wrong, then test it by having someone follow it without help. Wherever they get stuck, the SOP needs another line. Keep each one to a page or two, store them in one shared place, and review them when the process changes. SOPs are also the instructions you'll later give an AI agent, so good ones pay off twice.

Ask a small business owner how they send an invoice, onboard a new customer or publish a blog post, and you'll get a different answer each time. The process works, mostly, because they remember it. The moment they hand it over, to you, a new hire or an AI tool, it falls apart.

That's why standard operating procedures (SOPs) matter, and why so few small businesses have them. Writing them takes time. AI changes that maths completely, and it gives virtual assistants a very sellable service.

Why do SOPs matter more once a business uses AI?

Because an SOP is just a set of instructions, and instructions are exactly what AI needs. A well-written SOP for sorting a client's inbox is most of the way to being the standing instructions for an AI system that does the first pass. Businesses that document their processes can hand work to people and to AI far more easily than businesses that don't.

For a VA, that means SOPs aren't admin. They're the foundation of every AI service you'll sell, such as the inbox and calendar system or the task you pitch to a current client.

What should an SOP include?

Use the same format every time, so the client's SOPs read like a set rather than a pile:

  1. Name and purpose: what the process is and why it matters, in one or two lines.
  2. Owner and frequency: who does it, and when (daily, on each new order, at month end).
  3. What you need: the apps, logins (where they're stored, never the passwords themselves), templates and files.
  4. Steps: numbered, one action per step, starting with a verb. "Open the invoices folder", not "the invoices folder should be opened".
  5. Decisions: the if-then points. "If the amount is over $500, send it to the client to approve."
  6. Checks: how you know it's done right.
  7. When it goes wrong: the two or three most common problems and what to do.
  8. Last reviewed: a date and a name.

Aim for one to two pages. If it's longer, it's probably two processes.

How do you write an SOP with AI?

  1. Record it once. Do the task, or watch the client do it, with a screen recording running and talk through what you're doing and why. A meeting recorder works too. Ten minutes is usually enough.
  2. Get the transcript. Most recording tools produce one automatically.
  3. Draft with AI. Paste the transcript and the format above, and ask for a draft (prompt 1 below). This takes a couple of minutes.
  4. Fix it. AI is good at structure and bad at knowing what it wasn't told. Read every step and correct anything wrong, vague or missing.
  5. Find the gaps. Ask AI to read the draft as a newcomer and list every question they'd have (prompt 2). Answer them in the SOP.
  6. Test it cold. Have someone who hasn't done the task follow it without help. Wherever they stop and ask, add a line.
  7. Get sign-off. The client approves it, and it goes in the shared SOP folder.

The whole thing takes 20 to 40 minutes per process, against two hours or more writing from a blank page.

Where should a client's SOPs live?

In one place everyone can find, usually a shared folder in Google Drive or a page in Notion, with a simple index page listing each SOP, its owner and when it was last reviewed. Don't scatter them across email threads. Store passwords in a password manager and point to it from the SOP; never put them in the document itself.

How do you keep SOPs up to date?

  • Update on change. When a tool, price or step changes, update the SOP the same day. AI can rewrite the affected steps from a one-line description of the change.
  • Review quarterly. Go through the index every three months and check each SOP's date.
  • Watch for workarounds. If people keep doing a step differently, the SOP is wrong, not the people.

Can you sell SOP writing as a service?

Yes, and it's one of the easiest AI services to explain. Many business owners know they should have SOPs and never get round to them. Common ways to package it:

  • A starter set: the five processes the business depends on most, written, tested and stored, for a fixed price.
  • Per process: a set fee per SOP, with a discount for five or more.
  • Upkeep: a small monthly amount to keep them current and add new ones.

It also leads naturally to the next sale. Once a process is written down, you can show the client which steps AI could take over.

Which prompts should you use?

The first turns a recording into a draft:

1

Draft an SOP from a transcript

Prompt

Here's a transcript of someone doing a task and explaining it as they go: [paste]. Turn it into a standard operating procedure using exactly this format: name and purpose; owner and frequency; what you need; numbered steps (one action each, starting with a verb); decisions (if-then); checks; when it goes wrong (the most likely problems and what to do); last reviewed. Step 1: Use only what's in the transcript. Step 2: Where something is unclear or missing, write [CHECK: question] instead of guessing. Step 3: Never include passwords or account numbers; write "see password manager". Step 4: Keep it under two pages.

The second finds the gaps before a real person does:

2

Find the gaps in an SOP

Prompt

Read this SOP as if you were a new assistant doing the task for the first time with no one to ask: [paste]. Step 1: List every point where you'd get stuck, guess or need to ask a question, with the step number. Step 2: List any step that contains more than one action and should be split. Step 3: List anything that could go wrong that the SOP doesn't cover. Step 4: Suggest the exact wording to fix each problem. Don't rewrite the whole SOP.

The third shows the client what to hand to AI next:

3

Spot the steps AI could take over

Prompt

Here is an SOP for one of my client's processes: [paste]. Step 1: For each step, say whether it's best done by a person, by AI with a person checking, or by a simple automation, and why in one line. Step 2: Estimate roughly how much time the AI and automation steps could save each time the process runs. Step 3: Flag any step involving money, legal, personal or sensitive data that should stay with a person. Step 4: Suggest the one change with the best time saving and lowest risk to try first.

Start with one process you already do for a client every week. Record it next time you do it. By the end of the day you'll have your first SOP, and a sample to show every other client.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI write an SOP?

AI can draft one quickly from a transcript of someone doing the task, using a fixed format. A person still needs to correct it, fill the gaps and test it by having someone follow it without help, because AI only knows what it was told.

What should a standard operating procedure include?

A name and purpose, who owns it and how often it runs, what you need, numbered steps with one action each, the decision points, checks that it was done right, what to do when common problems happen, and the date it was last reviewed.

How long should an SOP be?

One to two pages for most small-business processes. If it runs longer, it usually covers more than one process and should be split.

What's the fastest way to create SOPs for a client?

Record the task once with someone talking through it, get the transcript, have AI draft the SOP in a fixed format, then fix and test it. This typically takes 20 to 40 minutes per process.

Can virtual assistants charge for writing SOPs?

Yes. Common packages are a fixed-price starter set of the five most important processes, a per-SOP fee, and a small monthly amount to keep them current. SOP work also shows the client which steps AI could take over next.

Where should SOPs be stored?

In one shared place such as a Google Drive folder or a Notion page, with an index listing each SOP, its owner and its review date. Keep passwords in a password manager, never inside the SOP.
