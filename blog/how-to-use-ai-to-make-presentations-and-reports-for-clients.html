How do virtual assistants use AI to make presentations and reports?

"Can you turn this into a few slides?" usually arrives with a messy folder, a short deadline and no clear point. AI can make slides fast. What makes them worth presenting is still a clear message, real numbers and a design that looks like the client's business. That's where a VA comes in.

By Denise Cullom · November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

Before you open any slide tool, agree on the audience and the one message: what they should know or do at the end. Ask AI for an outline first, one line per slide, and get the client's OK on it. Then have AI draft each slide or section from the client's real material, not general knowledge. Put it into the client's own template and brand, keep each slide to one point, and check every number, chart and name against the source. For reports that repeat, such as a monthly update, build the template once and fill it each month.

Presentations and reports are a steady source of work for VAs: sales decks, board updates, training slides, monthly reports, proposals for a client's own customers. Many AI tools can now build slides or documents from a prompt. The output often looks finished but says little. The method below gets you the speed of AI and a result your client is happy to put their name on.

What do you need before you start?

  1. The audience: who will see it, and what they already know.
  2. The one message: the sentence you want them to remember, or the decision you want them to make.
  3. The format: presented live, sent to read, or both. Slides to read need more words than slides to speak over.
  4. The length: number of slides or pages, and the time slot.
  5. The material: notes, past decks, figures, reports.
  6. The template and brand rules.

If the client can't give you the one message, help them find it. Ask: "If they forget everything else, what should they remember?" That answer becomes the title slide.

Why outline before you design?

Changing an outline takes a minute. Changing twenty finished slides takes an afternoon. Ask AI for an outline with one line per slide: the headline (a full sentence that makes the point, not a label like "Q3 results") and what goes on it. Read the headlines in order. They should tell the whole story on their own. Send the outline to the client and get their OK before you build anything.

How do you draft from the client's material?

Give AI the approved outline and the client's material, and ask it to draft the content for each slide using only that material. Ask it to mark any slide where the material was too thin, rather than filling the gap with general statements. Those gaps are questions for the client.

For speaker notes, ask for short notes in the client's voice. If you've written for them before, you have their voice rules already (see using AI without sounding like AI). Before pasting financial figures or customer details into an AI tool, check the client is comfortable with that, as covered in keeping client data safe.

How do you make it look like the client's business?

Use the client's own template, fonts and colors, not the AI tool's default theme. If they don't have a template, build a simple one once: a title slide, a section slide, a text slide, a chart slide and a closing slide, in their logo colors. Every deck after that starts from it.

  • One point per slide. If a slide needs two headlines, it's two slides.
  • Few words. For live talks, the speaker says the rest.
  • Real images of the client's team, products or work beat stock photos and AI-generated images.
  • Simple charts with the takeaway in the headline: "Repeat bookings up a third since March," not "Bookings chart."

How do you check the numbers?

This is the step that protects the client. Go through every figure, percentage, date, chart and name and check it against the source document, not against the AI draft. AI can round, mix up columns, or calculate a percentage wrongly. Recalculate any number AI worked out. Check that each chart's labels and totals match the data. Then read the headlines one last time, in order, to make sure the story still holds.

A short note to the client, "every figure checked against the March sales report," tells them what they're paying for.

How do you make a monthly report you can reuse?

Many clients need the same report every month: sales, bookings, website visits, social results, project status. Build it once: the same sections in the same order, a headline per section stating what changed, and a short "what we'll do next month" at the end. Each month, drop in the new figures, ask AI to draft the headlines and comments from the change since last month, and check them. A report like this turns into a monthly retainer, and it's also a natural place to show your own results (see showing clients the time AI saves).

Which prompts should you use?

The first builds the outline:

1

Outline the deck

Prompt

I'm making a presentation for my client. Audience: [who and what they know]. The one message: [sentence]. Format: [live or to read], [number] slides, [time slot]. Material: [paste or summarize]. Write an outline with one line per slide: a headline that is a full sentence making the point, then what goes on the slide. Reading only the headlines in order should tell the whole story. Use only the material I gave you.

The second drafts the content:

2

Draft the slides from the material

Prompt

Here is the approved outline: [paste]. Here is the client's material: [paste]. For each slide, write the headline, up to three short bullet points or one chart description, and speaker notes of up to 60 words in the client's voice: [voice notes]. Use only facts and figures from the material. Where the material doesn't support a slide, write "NEED FROM CLIENT:" and what's missing instead of filling the gap.

The third helps you check the figures:

3

List every figure to check

Prompt

Read this draft deck or report: [paste text]. List every number, percentage, date, name and chart in a table with: slide or page, the exact figure as written, and where it should come from in the source. Mark any figure that was calculated rather than copied, so I can recalculate it. Don't correct anything. Just give me the checklist.

Keep every deck you make in the client's folder with its outline. Next quarter's version will take half the time.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI make a presentation for a client?

AI can outline a deck and draft the slides quickly, but a VA should agree the audience and one message first, draft only from the client's real material, put it in the client's template, and check every number before it is shared.

Why write an outline before making slides?

Because changing an outline takes a minute and changing finished slides takes hours. An outline of full-sentence headlines shows whether the story works and lets the client approve it before anything is built.

How do you check numbers in an AI-drafted report?

Compare every figure, percentage, date, chart and name with the source document rather than the draft, and recalculate anything the AI worked out itself. Check chart labels and totals against the data.

Should you use AI-generated images in client presentations?

Real photos of the client's team, products or work are usually better. Use the client's template, fonts and colors instead of the AI tool's default design.

Can a VA sell monthly reports as a service?

Yes. Build the report template once, then each month add the new figures, draft headlines and comments with AI, check them, and deliver. It works well as a monthly retainer.
