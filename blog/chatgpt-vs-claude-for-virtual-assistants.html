ChatGPT vs. Claude: which is better for virtual assistants?

It's the question every VA asks once they decide to get serious about AI. The honest answer is that both are good enough, and the choice matters less than people think. But there are real differences in how they fit VA work, and here they are.

By Denise Cullom · November 6, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

For most virtual assistant work, both ChatGPT and Claude are excellent, and both cost about $20 a month for a paid individual plan. The practical differences: Claude tends to write client emails and documents that sound more natural with less editing, and it's strong at following a long set of standing instructions and working through long documents. ChatGPT has the broadest set of built-in features, including image creation and voice, and it's the tool more clients have already heard of. The best rule: if the client already pays for one, use that one. Otherwise, run the same real task through both for an hour and keep the one whose output you'd send with the fewest edits. Then learn it deeply instead of switching.

If you're new to this, read the best AI tools for virtual assistants first. The short version is that your AI assistant is the center of your whole stack, which is why this choice feels big. It's less big than it feels, because both tools are capable and both keep improving. But they do have different strengths.

Features and prices change often in both, so treat this as a guide to what to look for and check the details on each tool's own site.

What's the short answer for VAs?

  • If your client already pays for one, use it. The work stays in their account, under their agreement, and you avoid a new data conversation.
  • If most of your work is writing in a client's voice (emails, support replies, newsletters, SOPs), try Claude first.
  • If you need a wide range of features in one place (images, voice conversations, lots of add-ons), try ChatGPT first.
  • Either way, pick one, build your client instructions in it, and stop comparing.

Which writes better client emails?

This matters most, because so much VA work ends up as words a client signs. In my experience Claude's first drafts tend to sound more like a real person and need fewer edits to remove the giveaway AI phrases. ChatGPT is very capable too, and with a good voice guide in its instructions the gap gets small.

The bigger factor is your instructions, not the tool. Give either one a short voice guide, two or three real examples of the client's writing and a list of words to avoid, and the output improves more than switching tools would. The rules in using AI without sounding like AI work in both.

Which is better with long documents and instructions?

Both can read long files: contracts, policy manuals, a year of meeting notes, a full FAQ. Claude has a strong reputation for staying faithful to long documents and following detailed standing instructions without drifting, which suits workflows like support drafts written from a client's policies, or SOPs written from a long transcript. ChatGPT handles these jobs well too.

Whichever you choose, check the AI against the source. Neither tool is perfect at quoting documents, and the VA who catches the one wrong refund policy is the one who keeps the client.

Can both save instructions for each client?

Yes. Both let you create a project for each client, with standing instructions and reference files (the voice guide, the FAQ, the price list) that every chat inside it uses. This is the single most important feature for VA work. One well-built project per client is what turns AI from a novelty into a system.

Keep a copy of every project's instructions in a document of your own as well. If you ever change tools, or the client moves to a business account, you can rebuild it in minutes.

Which connects to the client's apps?

Both can now connect to common apps, such as email, calendars and cloud drives, and both can run some tasks on a schedule. That's what makes “every morning, sort the inbox and draft the routine replies” possible without a separate automation tool. The exact list of connections differs and changes often, so check that the specific apps your client uses are supported before you promise a workflow.

If a client lives entirely in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, also look at the AI built into those suites. It works inside the client's existing account, which can make the data conversation much easier.

What about images, voice and everything else?

ChatGPT has the broader feature set: image creation and editing, voice conversations and a large ecosystem of add-ons. If a client needs simple social graphics or quick mockups, that's useful. Claude is focused on text, documents, files and analysis, and for graphics you'd pair it with a design tool like Canva.

For most VA services this isn't decisive. Inbox triage, meeting follow-ups, reports and support drafts are text jobs.

How do they compare on cost and data settings?

  • Cost: both have a free tier and a paid individual plan at about $20 a month, with more expensive tiers for heavy use and team or business plans with admin controls.
  • Usage limits: both cap how much you can use a paid plan in a period. If you're running workflows for several clients, watch for limits and consider a higher tier or having each client pay for their own account.
  • Training on your data: both have a setting that controls whether your chats can be used to improve their models. Set it before any client work, and know that team and business plans generally keep it off by default.

Can a VA use both?

Yes, and many end up doing so because different clients pay for different tools. That's fine. What doesn't work is splitting your own daily practice between them and never getting deep in either. Have one home tool you know inside out and be competent in the other.

How do you decide in one hour?

Take one real task you do every week, with private details removed, and run it through both. Use this prompt in each:

1

Run the same test in both tools

Prompt

You are the assistant to [describe the client: business type, size, who their customers are]. Their writing voice: [three or four rules, for example short sentences, warm but direct, no exclamation marks]. Here are two examples of emails they've written: [paste]. Here is this week's input: [paste the emails, transcript or notes, with private details removed]. Step 1: [the job, for example sort these emails into urgent, needs reply, FYI and ignore, with one line each on why]. Step 2: Draft replies to the routine ones in the client's voice, under 100 words each. Step 3: List anything you were unsure about and would want a human to check.

Then score the two results honestly:

2

Score the results side by side

Prompt

I ran the same VA task through two AI tools. Here is the task and the client's voice rules: [paste]. Here is output A: [paste]. Here is output B: [paste]. Step 1: Score each from 1 to 5 on accuracy against the input, following the instructions, sounding like the client, and catching things a human should check. Step 2: List every edit I'd need to make to each before sending it. Step 3: Tell me which one needs less of my time to get to send-ready, and why. Don't tell me which tool produced which; judge only the text.

Whichever wins on your real work is the right one for you. Then commit to it for at least three months and build every client's project there.

Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT or Claude better for virtual assistants?

Both handle core VA work well. Claude tends to write more natural client emails and follows long standing instructions and documents well; ChatGPT has the broadest built-in features, including images and voice. Use the one your client already pays for, or test both on one real task and keep the one that needs fewer edits.

How much do ChatGPT and Claude cost?

Both have a free tier and a paid individual plan at about $20 a month, plus higher tiers for heavy use and team or business plans with admin controls. Check each tool's pricing page, because plans change.

Can ChatGPT and Claude remember instructions for each client?

Yes. Both let you create a project per client with standing instructions and reference files, such as a voice guide and FAQ, that every chat in the project uses. Keep a copy of the instructions in your own document too.

Which AI is better at writing in a client's voice?

Many people find Claude's drafts sound more natural with less editing, but either tool improves far more from a short voice guide, two or three real writing samples and a list of words to avoid than from switching tools.

Can a virtual assistant use both ChatGPT and Claude?

Yes. Different clients often pay for different tools. Have one home tool you know deeply and be competent in the other, rather than splitting your daily practice evenly.

Is it safe to use ChatGPT or Claude with client data?

With the right setup. Turn off the setting that allows your chats to be used for model training, or use a team or business plan, never paste passwords, full financial or ID numbers or health details, and have a human check anything before it goes out.

Which AI tool should I learn first as a new AI VA?

Whichever your current clients use. If they don't use either, run one real weekly task through both for an hour and pick the one whose output you'd send with the fewest edits, then stick with it for at least three months.
