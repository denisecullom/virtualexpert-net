How do you become an AI virtual assistant?

An AI virtual assistant isn't a robot and isn't a new job title you need a degree for. It's a VA who knows how to make AI do part of a client's work, safely, every week. Here's how to become one, starting with the clients you already have.

By Denise Cullom · November 4, 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

An AI virtual assistant is a VA who uses AI to deliver more, and who can set up and run AI routines for a client's business. To become one: use one AI assistant (ChatGPT or Claude) every day on your own work for two weeks, then pick one repetitive task a current client complains about, build a simple AI workflow for it, measure the hours it saves, and charge a setup fee plus a monthly fee to run it. You don't need to code. The skills that matter are writing clear instructions, checking AI output, keeping client data safe and explaining the results in hours and dollars. Most VAs can land a first paid AI job within 30 days by starting with a client who already trusts them.

A lot of virtual assistants are worried that AI will take their work. The ones who are doing well have noticed something different: their clients know they should be using AI, and almost none of them have the time or patience to work out how. They'd happily pay someone they already trust to do it for them.

That someone can be you. This article is the map.

What is an AI virtual assistant?

An AI virtual assistant is a human VA who uses AI tools to get more done, and who can set up AI to handle recurring parts of a client's business: sorting the inbox, writing meeting follow-ups, drafting support replies, preparing the weekly numbers. The VA stays in charge. They write the instructions, check what the AI produces, fix it when it goes wrong and report what it's saving.

The phrase sometimes gets used for AI chatbots themselves. That's not what clients mean when they ask for help. They want a person who makes the AI work for their business, and who is accountable when it doesn't.

Will AI replace virtual assistants?

AI will replace some tasks virtual assistants do today, especially the ones that are pure copy, paste and reformat. It's much less good at the parts that keep clients loyal: knowing the business, knowing who matters, noticing what's off, and taking responsibility for getting things done.

The VAs most at risk are the ones billing by the hour for tasks AI now does in seconds. The VAs who gain are the ones who move from doing those tasks to running the AI that does them, and who get paid for the result instead of the time.

What are the three levels of AI VA work?

  1. AI-assisted VA. You use AI on your own work: drafts, summaries, research, cleanup. You deliver faster, but the client may not see a difference except speed.
  2. AI setup VA. You build something for the client: an AI routine with instructions, templates and a schedule that does one job in their business every week. You charge a setup fee.
  3. AI operator VA. You run several of these routines for the client, check their output, improve them and report the time they save each month. You charge a monthly fee to keep them running.

Every level builds on the one before. Don't try to sell level 2 work until you've spent a couple of weeks at level 1 and trust the tools yourself.

What skills does an AI virtual assistant need?

Less technical than you'd think. The five that matter most:

  • Writing clear instructions. AI does what you tell it, so the skill is telling it well: who it's acting as, what it's working from, the steps to follow and the format you want back. The structure is the same one in writing AI prompts that work.
  • Checking output. Knowing what good looks like for this client, and catching the made-up fact, the wrong tone or the missed exception before the client sees it.
  • Spotting the right tasks. Repetitive, rule-based, frequent and annoying. Not the one-off judgment calls.
  • Keeping data safe. Knowing what never goes into an AI tool and whose account the work should run in.
  • Explaining results. Turning “I set up a thing” into “this saves you six hours a month.” That sentence is what clients pay for.

You'll also need to keep the AI from sounding like AI when it writes for a client. The voice rules in using AI without sounding like AI apply just as much to a client's emails as to your own.

Do you need to know how to code?

No. Almost everything a small business needs from an AI VA runs inside a regular AI assistant with a saved set of instructions, plus the apps the client already uses. Automation tools like Zapier help with some jobs, and they're point-and-click. If you can use email, Google Docs and a spreadsheet confidently, you have the technical base you need.

What are the client data rules?

This is the part that separates a professional from someone who's going to get a client in trouble. Agree these with every client before you start:

  • Never paste in passwords, full card or bank numbers, government ID numbers, or health details. Ever.
  • Check the training setting. Both ChatGPT and Claude have a setting that controls whether your chats can be used to improve their models. Know where it is and set it before any client work.
  • Prefer the client's account. Where possible, the AI work runs in an account the client owns, so the data and the history stay with their business if you part ways.
  • A human checks before it goes out. AI drafts; a person approves anything a customer, supplier or the public will see.
  • Put it in writing. A one-page AI use agreement that says what you'll use AI for, what you won't, and what happens if something goes wrong.

How do you get your first AI client?

Start with a client you already have. They trust you, you know their work, and you've probably heard them complain about exactly the task AI could take over. Don't pitch “AI.” Pitch the fix to the complaint:

  • “You mentioned email is eating your mornings. I can set up a routine that sorts it every morning and drafts the routine replies for you to approve.”
  • “You said you never get round to the follow-up after calls. I can set up something that turns each call into notes, action items and a draft email.”

Offer to build it, run it for a month, and show them the hours saved. If you have no clients yet, practice the whole thing on a realistic made-up business first, then use it as your first case study.

What does a 30-day plan look like?

  • Days 1 to 7: use it on yourself. Pick ChatGPT or Claude and use it every working day: drafts, summaries, research, rewriting. Keep a short note of what worked.
  • Days 8 to 14: choose the job. List every recurring task for one client. Score each on how often it happens, how rule-based it is and how much the client hates it. Pick the top one.
  • Days 15 to 21: build and test. Write the instructions, run it on last month's real examples (with private details removed) and fix it until you'd be happy to sign your name to the output.
  • Days 22 to 30: pitch and launch. Show the client a before and after, agree the data rules, set a price and start. Track the hours saved from day one.

How can AI help you get started?

Use this to find your first AI job inside the work you already do:

1

Find the first task to hand to AI

Prompt

Act as an operations consultant helping a virtual assistant add AI services. Here is a list of everything I do for one client in a typical month, with roughly how long each takes: [paste list, with no private client details]. The client is a [type of business] and has complained about [anything they've said]. Step 1: Score each task from 1 to 5 on how often it happens, how rule-based it is, how much time it takes, and how much the client dislikes it. Step 2: Show the top three in a table with the total score and why. Step 3: For the top one, describe in plain English how AI could do most of it every week, what I would still check by hand, and roughly how many hours a month it could save. Step 4: Write one sentence I could say to the client to suggest it, in terms of their complaint, not AI.

Then turn that task into a reusable set of instructions:

2

Write the instructions for your first workflow

Prompt

I'm setting up a recurring AI workflow for a client. The job is: [describe the task]. It runs [how often] and works from [what the input is, for example emails, a transcript, a spreadsheet]. Step 1: Ask me up to eight questions about the client's business, tone, rules and exceptions before you write anything. Step 2: When I've answered, write a complete set of standing instructions for an AI assistant to do this job: its role, what it should read, the exact steps, the format of the output, what it must never do, and when it should stop and flag something for a human. Step 3: Write a five-point checklist I can use to check each output before it reaches the client.

The first workflow is the hardest. Once you've built one, measured it and been paid for it, the second takes half the time, and you're no longer a VA who uses AI. You're the person clients call when they say “can you set up AI for us?”

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI virtual assistant?

A human virtual assistant who uses AI tools to get more done and who can set up and run AI routines for a client's business, such as inbox triage, meeting follow-ups or weekly reports. The VA writes the instructions, checks the output and is accountable for the results.

Will AI replace virtual assistants?

AI replaces some tasks, mostly copy, paste and reformat work. It doesn't replace knowing the client's business, judgment and accountability. VAs who learn to run AI for clients, and charge for results instead of hours, are in a stronger position than before.

Do I need to know how to code to become an AI virtual assistant?

No. Most small-business AI work runs inside a regular AI assistant like ChatGPT or Claude with saved instructions, plus the apps the client already uses. If you're comfortable with email, documents and spreadsheets, you have the technical base you need.

What skills do AI virtual assistants need?

Writing clear instructions for AI, checking AI output carefully, spotting which tasks suit AI, keeping client data safe, and explaining the results to the client in hours and dollars saved.

How long does it take to become an AI virtual assistant?

Most VAs can be using AI confidently on their own work within one to two weeks and land a first paid AI job within about 30 days, especially if they start with a client who already trusts them.

How do I find my first AI client as a VA?

Start with a current client. Listen for a recurring task they complain about, offer to set up an AI routine that fixes it, run it for a month and show them the hours saved. Pitch the fix to their problem, not “AI.”

Is it safe to use AI with client information?

It can be, with rules: never paste passwords, full financial or ID numbers, or health details; set the training setting before client work; run the work in the client's own account where possible; have a human approve anything that goes out; and put the rules in a short written agreement.
