How do virtual assistants use AI to help with a client's bookkeeping?

Most small business owners don't hate their bookkeeper. They hate the pile of receipts, the unpaid invoices and the month-end scramble before the bookkeeper can start. That pile is where a VA with AI earns their fee.

By Denise Cullom · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

A VA's job is bookkeeping prep, not bookkeeping. With AI, you can: list transactions with no receipt and draft the chaser messages, suggest an expense category for each transaction from the client's own chart of accounts for the bookkeeper to confirm, send polite invoice reminders on a set schedule, and write a plain-English month-end summary of money in, money out and anything unusual. The bookkeeper or accountant makes the final calls on categories, tax and reconciliation. Never put full bank or card numbers into AI, use read-only or limited access, and agree the boundaries in writing.

Bookkeeping prep is unglamorous work, and that's why it sells. The client has a bookkeeper or accountant, but every month they're asked for missing receipts, they're not sure who still owes them money, and they can't say in one sentence how the month went. A VA who keeps all of that tidy saves the owner hours and often saves on the bookkeeper's bill too.

What should a VA do, and not do, with a client's books?

Be clear about the line, with the client and with yourself:

  • A VA prepares: collecting receipts, matching them to transactions, suggesting categories, chasing unpaid invoices, organizing documents, summarizing the month.
  • The bookkeeper or accountant decides: final categories, reconciliations, tax treatment, payroll, filings and any financial advice.

Introduce yourself to the client's bookkeeper early. Ask how they like documents named and delivered. They're usually delighted, because you make their job easier.

How do you chase missing receipts with AI?

Each week, export the transactions from the bank feed or accounting app, and compare them with the receipts folder. AI lists every transaction with no matching receipt, then drafts the messages: one to the client listing what's missing, and one to any supplier who needs to resend an invoice. You check the list and send the messages. Doing this weekly instead of at month-end turns a scramble into a five-minute task.

How does AI help with expense categories?

Give the AI the client's own chart of accounts and a few months of transactions the bookkeeper has already categorized. For each new transaction, it suggests a category and says how sure it is. You pass the list to the bookkeeper as suggestions, with the unsure ones flagged. Over time, the bookkeeper corrects less, and you add their corrections to the instructions so the suggestions keep improving.

Never let AI-suggested categories go into the books unchecked. A wrong category can change a tax return.

How do you send invoice reminders with AI?

Agree a reminder schedule with the client, for example a friendly note three days before the due date, a reminder on the day, and firmer notes at 7 and 14 days overdue. Each week, AI checks the list of open invoices and drafts the right message for each, in the client's tone. You review and send. Anything over 30 days, or any customer who disputes the bill, goes back to the client. Faster reminders usually mean faster payment, which clients feel immediately in their bank balance.

What goes in a month-end summary?

One page, in plain English, for the owner, not the accountant:

  • Money in and money out this month, compared with last month.
  • The biggest expenses and anything unusual, such as a new subscription or a cost that doubled.
  • Who owes money and how much is overdue.
  • What's still missing before the bookkeeper can close the month.

Use the numbers from the accounting app or the bookkeeper's report, not AI arithmetic, and check every figure before you send it. AI is good at writing the summary. It's not the right tool for adding up the books.

How do you keep financial data safe?

Use read-only or limited user access to the accounting app, never the owner's login. Don't paste full card or bank numbers into AI; the last four digits are enough. Work in the client's own business AI account where you can, and keep their files in a separate project. The full checklist is in keeping client data safe when you use AI.

What should a VA charge for bookkeeping prep?

A setup fee for the receipt process, the category instructions and the reminder schedule, then a monthly fee. The Bookkeeping Prep Kit suggests a setup fee of $400 to $800, and the hours-saved calculator helps you show the value, which includes getting paid sooner, not just the hours.

Which prompts should you use?

The first finds missing receipts:

1

Find missing receipts and draft the chasers

Prompt

Here is this week's transaction list (date, payee, amount, last four digits only): [paste]. Here is the list of receipts I have: [paste file names or details]. Step 1: List every transaction with no matching receipt, matching on date, payee and amount. Step 2: Flag any near-matches I should check by hand. Step 3: Draft a short, friendly message to my client listing what's missing. Step 4: Draft a message to each supplier who needs to resend an invoice. Don't guess at matches you're unsure of.

The second suggests categories for the bookkeeper:

2

Suggest expense categories

Prompt

Here is my client's chart of accounts: [paste]. Here are past transactions the bookkeeper has already categorized: [paste]. For each new transaction below, suggest a category from the chart of accounts only, and rate your confidence as high, medium or low. Explain any low-confidence suggestion in one line. Present it as a table I can send to the bookkeeper as suggestions for them to confirm. New transactions: [paste]

The third writes the month-end summary:

3

Write the month-end summary

Prompt

Write a one-page month-end summary for a small business owner from these figures, which come from the accounting app: [paste money in, money out, biggest expenses, last month's figures, open invoices]. Sections: how the month went in two sentences, money in and out compared with last month, biggest and unusual expenses, who owes money, what's still missing for the bookkeeper. Use only these figures, don't calculate new totals, and write in plain English.

Start with the weekly receipt check. It's the smallest piece, the bookkeeper will notice the difference in the first month, and it gives you an easy way to introduce the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Can a virtual assistant do bookkeeping with AI?

A VA can do bookkeeping prep with AI: chasing missing receipts, suggesting expense categories, sending invoice reminders and writing a month-end summary. Final categories, reconciliation, tax and filings stay with the client's bookkeeper or accountant.

How do you use AI to find missing receipts?

Export the week's transactions and compare them with the receipts folder. AI lists the transactions with no matching receipt, flags near-matches to check by hand, and drafts messages to the client and to suppliers.

Can AI categorize business expenses?

AI can suggest categories from the client's own chart of accounts, based on past transactions the bookkeeper categorized, with a confidence rating. The bookkeeper should confirm every suggestion before it goes into the books.

How should a VA send invoice reminders?

Agree a schedule with the client, such as a note before the due date, one on the day and firmer ones at 7 and 14 days overdue. AI drafts each reminder in the client's tone, the VA reviews and sends, and anything over 30 days or disputed goes back to the client.

Is it safe to use AI with a client's financial data?

It can be if you use read-only or limited access to the accounting app, never the owner's login, keep full card and bank numbers out of AI, work in a business account the client controls, and use figures from the accounting app rather than AI arithmetic.

Bookkeeping prep is one of the AI services VAs can sell. If you're pitching it to a current client, start with this four-sentence pitch.
