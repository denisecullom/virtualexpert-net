How do virtual assistants use AI to manage a client's social media?

AI can produce a month of social posts in ten minutes. That's the problem: they all sound the same as everyone else's. Here's the workflow VAs use to get the speed without the generic posts, and keep the client's voice intact.

By Denise Cullom · November 7, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Start with a one-page brand voice guide: who the client talks to, how they sound, words they use and avoid, and three real posts that sound like them. Build each month's plan from the client's real material, such as a podcast episode, a newsletter, customer questions or a project they finished, never from AI's ideas alone. Then use AI to turn one piece of content into a week of posts for each platform, draft replies to comments for you to check, and write a short monthly report. The client approves the week's posts before they're scheduled. AI does the drafting and resizing; you supply the judgment, the real stories and the final check.

Social media is one of the most common jobs clients hand to a VA, and one of the first places people try AI. The results are usually fast and forgettable: posts full of "unlock", "elevate" and three rocket emojis, saying nothing a thousand other businesses haven't said that morning.

The fix isn't a better tool. It's giving AI two things it can't invent: the client's real voice and the client's real material.

Why do AI-written social posts sound so generic?

Because when you ask AI for "five posts about our bakery", it has nothing to go on except what bakery posts usually say. So it writes the average bakery post. The more specific the input (a real customer question, a real behind-the-scenes moment, the owner's actual opinion), the less average the output. The same applies to any writing, as covered in how to use AI without sounding like AI.

What goes in a brand voice guide?

One page, written with the client once and saved as standing instructions in your AI assistant:

  • Who they're talking to: one or two sentences about their ideal customer.
  • How they sound: three adjectives, with an example of each. "Warm, like talking to a regular at the counter."
  • Words they use: their phrases, product names, how they refer to customers.
  • Words they never use: including the usual AI giveaways.
  • Emoji and hashtag rules: how many, which ones, and where.
  • Topics to avoid: politics, competitors, anything legal or sensitive.
  • Three real posts that sound exactly right.

How do you plan a month of social content with AI?

  1. Collect the raw material. Before planning, gather what the client already has: blog posts, newsletters, podcast or video episodes, FAQs from customer emails, reviews, launches and events coming up, photos of real work.
  2. Pick three or four themes. For example: tips, behind the scenes, customer stories and offers. Keep offers to about one post in five.
  3. Map it out. AI turns the material and themes into a calendar: which piece of content feeds which week, and the dates that matter.
  4. Get the plan approved. A five-minute look from the client at the start of the month saves a lot of rewriting later.

How do you turn one piece of content into a week of posts?

This is where AI saves the most time. One newsletter, blog post or episode can become a week's posts across platforms:

  • A short post with the single most useful tip.
  • A carousel or thread that walks through the main points.
  • A quote or strong opinion pulled straight from the piece.
  • A question to the audience based on the topic.
  • A behind-the-scenes angle: why the client wrote it, or what happened.
  • A short video script, if the client does video.

AI drafts all of them, each sized for its platform. You edit for voice, check facts against the original, and choose the images. The client approves the week in one batch, then you schedule it in their scheduling tool.

Can AI reply to comments and messages?

It can draft replies, and you should check every one before it's posted. Give AI the voice guide and a short list of standard answers (opening hours, prices, how to book), and it can draft replies to routine comments quickly. Anything negative, personal, about a complaint or a refund, or from a journalist or partner goes to the client untouched. Never set up anything that replies automatically without a person seeing it first.

What should a monthly social media report include?

Keep it to one page the client will actually read:

  • Three numbers that matter for their goal, such as profile visits, link clicks or enquiries, compared with last month. Follower counts alone don't pay bills.
  • The best and worst post, and one sentence on why.
  • What you'll do differently next month.
  • Hours you spent, and hours you saved with the AI workflow.

AI can turn an export of the platform's numbers into this summary in a minute. Check every figure against the export before sending.

Which prompts should you use?

The first builds the voice guide from the client's own posts:

1

Build a brand voice guide

Prompt

Here are 10 social posts my client wrote that they're happy with: [paste]. Here's what they told me about their customers and style: [paste notes]. Step 1: Describe their voice in three adjectives, each with an example line from the posts. Step 2: List phrases they use often and words they never use. Step 3: Note their patterns: post length, how they open, emoji and hashtag use, how they end. Step 4: Write it up as a one-page voice guide I can save and reuse. Use only what's in the posts and notes.

The second turns one piece of content into a week of posts:

2

Turn one piece of content into a week of posts

Prompt

Using my client's voice guide [paste], turn this piece of content into a week of social posts: [paste the newsletter, blog post or transcript]. Platforms: [list]. Step 1: Pull out the five most useful or interesting points. Step 2: Write six posts: a tip, a carousel or thread outline, a strong quote, a question for the audience, a behind-the-scenes angle and one soft call to action to [link]. Step 3: Size each one for its platform. Step 4: Use only facts from the content; mark anything I should check with [CHECK]. No generic hashtags and no words from the never-use list.

The third writes the monthly report:

3

Write the monthly social report

Prompt

Here are my client's social numbers for this month and last month: [paste export]. Their goal for social media is [goal]. Step 1: Pick the three numbers that best show progress toward that goal and compare them with last month. Step 2: Name the best and worst post and suggest one reason for each. Step 3: Suggest two changes for next month. Step 4: Write it as a one-page plain-English report, numbers first, no jargon. Don't round or change any figure from the export.

The real skill here isn't writing posts anymore. It's knowing the client well enough to feed AI the right material and catch what doesn't sound like them. That's what clients pay a VA for, and it's much harder to replace than typing.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI manage social media for a small business?

AI can draft posts, resize content for each platform, draft comment replies and summarize monthly numbers. A person still needs to supply the real material, keep the client's voice, check facts and approve posts and replies before they go live.

How do you stop AI social posts sounding generic?

Give AI a brand voice guide with real example posts, and build posts from the client's real material, such as newsletters, customer questions and behind-the-scenes moments, rather than asking it to invent ideas. Then edit every draft for voice.

What is a brand voice guide?

A one-page document describing who the business talks to, how it sounds, words it uses and avoids, emoji and hashtag rules, topics to avoid, and three posts that sound exactly right. Saved as standing instructions, it keeps AI drafts on brand.

How do you repurpose one piece of content into social posts?

Pull the most useful points from one blog post, newsletter or episode, then turn them into a week of posts: a tip, a carousel or thread, a quote, a question, a behind-the-scenes angle and one call to action, each sized for its platform.

Should AI reply to social media comments automatically?

No. AI can draft replies to routine comments using standard answers, but a person should check each one before it's posted. Complaints, refunds, personal messages and press enquiries should go straight to the client.

What should be in a monthly social media report for a client?

Three numbers tied to the client's goal compared with last month, the best and worst post with a reason for each, two changes for next month, and the hours spent and saved. Keep it to one page.
