How should a virtual assistant onboard a new client when using AI?

Most clients decide whether they'll keep a VA in the first two weeks. A clear onboarding plan makes those weeks feel organized, calm and quick to pay off. AI makes the paperwork faster, as long as you set it up in the right order.

By Denise Cullom · November 10, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Run the same two-week plan for every new client. Day 1: send a short welcome pack (how you work, hours, how to reach you, what you need from them). Days 2 to 3: a 45-minute kickoff call, recorded with permission, covering their priorities, tools, tone and no-go areas. Days 3 to 5: set up access without passwords and sign a one-page AI data agreement. Days 4 to 7: turn the call into a client rulebook your AI tools follow, and time the tasks you'll take over so you have a baseline. Days 7 to 10: deliver one visible quick win. Day 14: a 20-minute check-in to confirm what's working and what's next.

A new client is hopeful and a little nervous. They've handed someone their inbox, their calendar or their customers, and they're watching to see whether it was a good idea. The fastest way to settle that is an onboarding plan that runs the same way every time, so nothing important depends on memory.

AI helps here in two ways: it turns your kickoff call into organized documents in minutes, and it gives you a "rulebook" that keeps every AI draft in the client's voice from the first week. Here's the plan, day by day.

What goes in a new client welcome pack?

Send it on day one, on one or two pages:

  • How you work: your hours, your reply times, and how you prefer requests (one shared list beats scattered messages).
  • What happens in the first two weeks, so they know what to expect and when.
  • What you need from them: the access list, a time for the kickoff call, and examples of emails or posts they like.
  • How you use AI, in two sentences, with a note that nothing goes out in their name without review.

What should you cover in the kickoff call?

Book 45 minutes and ask to record it so you can focus on listening. Cover:

  1. Their top three priorities for the next month, in their words.
  2. The tasks you're taking over, and roughly how long each takes them now.
  3. Their tools: email, calendar, documents, project and accounts apps.
  4. Their people: who gets a reply fast, who can wait, and who you should never contact.
  5. Their tone: how they open and close emails, words they'd never use.
  6. No-go areas: anything you shouldn't touch, send or decide without asking.
  7. How they want updates: daily note, weekly summary or only when needed.

How do you set up access safely?

Use delegated access, shared folders and your own user on their apps, never their password. Then sign a one-page agreement covering which AI tools you use, what each can see and what never goes in. The full checklist is in keeping client data safe when you use AI. Doing this in the first week shows the client you take their data seriously before they've had to ask.

What is a client rulebook, and how do you write it?

A client rulebook is one document that tells your AI tools how this client works: their priorities, their key people, their tone, their no-go areas and their standard answers to common questions. Put it in the project or custom instructions for that client, and every draft starts from it.

Write it from the kickoff call. Give the AI the transcript, ask for a first draft in the format below, then correct it yourself. Show it to the client and ask "is anything wrong or missing?" That one question catches most of the mistakes you'd otherwise find the hard way. The inbox and calendar guide shows a rulebook in action.

Why measure before you automate?

Before you switch any AI system on, time the tasks you're taking over for three or four days. That gives you the "before" number you'll need for your monthly results report. Once the AI system is running, the old time is gone and you can't measure it again.

What makes a good quick win?

Something the client sees and feels by day ten. Good examples: an inbox down from hundreds to zero with a short summary of what's waiting for them, a tidy calendar with prep notes for next week's meetings, or a list of unpaid invoices with reminder drafts ready to send. Pick the task they complained about most on the kickoff call. One clear win early does more for the relationship than a month of quiet, steady work.

What happens at the two-week check-in?

Twenty minutes. Three questions: what's working, what isn't, and what would they hand over next? Update the rulebook with anything new, confirm the baseline numbers, and agree when your first monthly report will arrive. Then you're out of onboarding and into the ongoing work.

Which prompts should you use?

The first writes the welcome pack:

1

Write the welcome pack

Prompt

Write a welcome pack for my new client, [name, business]. I'm their virtual assistant, taking over [tasks]. My hours are [hours], I reply within [time], and I prefer requests in [tool]. Step 1: Write a friendly opening paragraph. Step 2: Lay out the first two weeks: welcome pack, kickoff call, access setup, rulebook, quick win, two-week check-in. Step 3: List what I need from them: [access list], a kickoff time, and examples of emails they like. Step 4: Add two sentences on how I use AI, including that nothing goes out in their name without my review. Keep it under one page.

The second turns the kickoff call into a rulebook:

2

Turn the kickoff call into a client rulebook

Prompt

Here is the transcript of my kickoff call with a new client: [paste]. Write a client rulebook my AI tools will follow. Sections: top priorities, tasks I'm taking over and how long each takes now, tools, key people (reply fast / can wait / never contact), tone (openings, sign-offs, words to avoid), no-go areas, standard answers to common questions, how they want updates. Use only what's in the transcript. Where something is missing or unclear, write [ask client] instead of guessing. Then list my open questions for the client.

The third picks the quick win:

3

Choose the day-ten quick win

Prompt

Using this client rulebook and these baseline timings: [paste], suggest three quick wins I could deliver within ten days. For each: what I'd do, roughly how long it takes me, what the client will see at the end, and how it links to what they complained about most. Rank them by visible impact for the least effort, and recommend one. Then write a two-line message telling the client what I'm starting on.

Save your welcome pack, kickoff questions and rulebook format as templates. After the second client, onboarding takes a fraction of the time, and every new client gets the same organized start.

Frequently asked questions

How should a virtual assistant onboard a new client?

Follow the same two-week plan every time: a welcome pack on day one, a 45-minute kickoff call, safe access and a one-page AI data agreement, a client rulebook for your AI tools, a time baseline before automating, one quick win by day ten, and a check-in at day fourteen.

What questions should a VA ask in a kickoff call?

Ask about the client's top three priorities, the tasks you're taking over and how long they take now, their tools, their key people, their tone, their no-go areas, and how they want updates.

What is a client rulebook for AI?

One document that tells your AI tools how a client works: priorities, key people, tone, no-go areas and standard answers. You put it in that client's project or custom instructions so every draft starts in their voice.

How long should VA client onboarding take?

About two weeks: the first week for the welcome pack, kickoff, access and rulebook, the second for the baseline and a visible quick win, ending with a short check-in.

What is a good quick win for a new VA client?

Something visible within ten days that fixes the task they complained about most, such as an inbox brought to zero with a summary, a calendar with prep notes, or unpaid invoices listed with reminder drafts ready.

Onboarding is where you set the baseline for your monthly results report, so don't skip the timing step.
