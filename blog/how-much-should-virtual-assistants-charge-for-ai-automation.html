How much should virtual assistants charge for AI automation?

Here's the trap: AI makes you faster, so if you keep billing by the hour, every improvement you make cuts your pay. The fix is to price AI work on what you build and what it saves, not on how long it takes you.

By Denise Cullom · November 8, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

Price AI automation in two parts. A setup fee pays for building and testing the workflow: a simple formula is hours to build and test × your hourly rate × 1.5. A monthly fee pays for running, checking and improving it: anchor it to value, at roughly a quarter of what the time saved is worth to the client. For a US small business, typical starting points are $250 to $900 setup and $100 to $600 a month per workflow, or packages from about $300 + $150/month for one workflow to $1,500 + $750/month for five or more. Don't bill AI work by the hour: if a five-hour job now takes one, hourly billing cuts your pay by 80% for doing better work.

If you've picked a service from the ten AI services VAs can sell, the next question is what to charge. For pricing in general (fixed versus hourly, floors and proposals), see how to price your services. This article is about the specific problem AI creates.

Why shouldn't VAs charge hourly for AI work?

Because the better you get, the less you earn. Say you spend five hours a week on a client's inbox at $35 an hour: $175 a week. You set up an AI routine that does most of it, and now it takes you one hour to check and send. At the same hourly rate you just took an 80% pay cut, and the client is getting a better service than before.

Clients don't actually want to buy your hours. They want the inbox handled. Price the outcome, and getting faster becomes your profit instead of your loss.

How do you set the setup fee?

The setup fee pays for building the workflow: learning the job, writing the instructions, connecting the tools, testing on real examples, fixing what's wrong and showing the client how it works. A simple formula:

Setup fee = hours to build and test × your hourly rate × 1.5

The 1.5 covers the learning you did to be able to build it at all, and the fact that the client gets something that keeps working after you've left. If you estimate six hours at $40, that's $240 × 1.5 = $360.

Estimate hours honestly the first few times, and track them. Your first build of a workflow always takes longer than your fifth.

How do you set the monthly fee?

The monthly fee pays for running it: checking the output, fixing what breaks, updating instructions as the business changes and reporting results. Anchor it to the value, not to your time:

Monthly value = hours saved per month × what an hour of that time is worth to the client
Monthly fee ≈ a quarter of that value

Use the client's own value of the time, not yours. If the owner is doing the work, their time is worth what they could bill or sell in that hour. If a staff member is, use their pay plus overheads. A fee of around a quarter of the value is an easy yes: the client keeps three quarters of the savings.

What does a worked example look like?

A consultant who bills $150 an hour spends about 8 hours a month writing up meeting notes and follow-up emails. You set up a meeting follow-up workflow.

  • Setup: 5 hours to build and test at your $40 rate × 1.5 = $300.
  • Value: the workflow saves about 6 of those 8 hours. 6 × $150 = $900 a month of the consultant's time back.
  • Monthly fee: about a quarter of $900, so $225 a month. Round to $200 or $250 depending on your market.
  • Your time to run it: about 90 minutes a month to check and send. That's well over $100 an hour for you, and the client is $675 a month better off.

Everybody wins, which is why this pricing holds up when the client thinks about it.

What are good starting packages?

Clients find packages easier to say yes to than a list of fees. Starting points for US small businesses:

  • AI Starter: one workflow set up and run. About $300 setup + $150 a month.
  • AI Office: three workflows set up and run, with a monthly results report. About $900 setup + $400 a month.
  • AI Operator: five or more workflows, a monthly review and a new workflow each quarter. About $1,500 setup + $750 a month.

Adjust to your market and experience, but keep the shape: one-time setup plus a monthly fee. The monthly fee is what turns AI work into dependable income.

Who pays for the AI tools?

Decide this before you quote. Two clean options:

  • The client pays directly. The AI or automation account is in their name and on their card. Best for data ownership, and the cost is out of your price entirely.
  • You include it. You use your own account and build the cost into the monthly fee. Simpler for the client, but watch usage limits as you add clients.

For anything touching sensitive client data, the first option is usually better.

How do you raise prices with current clients?

Don't announce a price rise. Offer something new. Your existing hourly work stays as it is; the AI workflow is a new service with its own price. A month in, show the client the hours saved. Once they've seen the number, moving more of your work to the setup-and-monthly model is an easy conversation:

“The meeting follow-up has saved you about six hours this month. I'd like to set up the same kind of thing for your inbox. It'd be a one-time setup of $400, then $200 a month, and I'd send you the same report so you can see what it's saving.”

What if a client says “I could just do this myself with ChatGPT”?

Agree with them. They could. They could also do their own bookkeeping. What you're selling is that it's built properly, it works every week, someone checks it before anything goes out, it gets fixed when it breaks, and they never have to think about it. If they'd rather learn it themselves, offer a one-off training session instead. Some will come back once they've tried.

How can AI help you price your services?

1

Price one AI workflow

Prompt

Help me price an AI workflow for a client. The workflow: [describe it]. The client: [type of business], and the person who currently does this task is [owner or role] whose time is worth about [$ per hour, or their billing rate]. They spend about [hours] a month on it now. I estimate the workflow will save about [hours] a month and take me [hours] to build and test, and [hours] a month to run. My hourly rate is [$]. Step 1: Calculate the setup fee as build hours x my rate x 1.5. Step 2: Calculate the monthly value to the client and a monthly fee of about a quarter of that. Step 3: Show my effective hourly rate for running it. Step 4: Flag anything in my estimates that looks unrealistic. Step 5: Write the two-sentence price I'd say out loud to the client.

2

Write the results report that justifies the fee

Prompt

Write a short monthly results report from me, a virtual assistant, to my client for the AI workflows I run for them. Here's what happened this month: [list each workflow, what it handled, for example 212 emails sorted and 64 replies drafted, any problems fixed, and estimated hours saved]. The client's time is worth about [$ per hour]. Step 1: Lead with total hours saved and their dollar value in one sentence. Step 2: One short line per workflow. Step 3: One thing I improved or fixed this month. Step 4: One suggestion for next month. Keep it under 200 words, plain and warm, no jargon, in a format I can paste into an email.

The report is what keeps the monthly fee safe. Clients rarely cancel something when they can see what it saves them every month.

How do I calculate a setup fee for an AI workflow?

Multiply the hours it will take you to build and test the workflow by your hourly rate, then by 1.5. The extra half covers the skill you've built and the fact that the client keeps a working system.

How do I set a monthly fee for AI automation?
