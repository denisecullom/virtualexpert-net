Your free prompts

15 AI prompts to land your next client

Each prompt gives your AI assistant a role, the context it needs, a step-by-step task and the format you want back, so you get answers you can actually use. They follow the five stages of landing a client, from finding the right people to a signed yes.

How to use them

  1. Fill in every [bracket]. Paste real details: your past clients, their words, your prices.
  2. Run them in one conversation, in order. Prompt 1 defines your client and prompt 4 teaches AI your voice; every prompt after that builds on both.
  3. AI drafts, you decide. Edit everything so it sounds like you before you send it. Never paste private client data.

Find

  1. 01

    Find your best-fit client

    Find

    Use it when you're not sure who to go after, or you say yes to anyone.

    You are a sales strategist who helps independent professionals pick a niche they can win in. About me: I'm a [your role] who helps [type of client] with [result]. My three favorite past clients or projects were: 1. [who they were, what I did, what changed for them] 2. [same] 3. [same] Step 1: List what these clients have in common (industry, size, stage, the decision maker's role, the trigger that made them look for help). Step 2: Describe my ideal client in 6 bullet points: who they are, the problem that costs them money or time, what they've already tried, what they'd pay to fix it, where they spend time, and the words they use to describe the problem. Step 3: Name 2 client types I should stop chasing, and why. Before you answer, ask me up to 3 questions if anything important is missing.

    Then: Save the 6 bullets. Every prompt below says "my ideal client"; paste these bullets there.

  2. 02

    Turn your offer into a sentence people remember

    Find

    Use it when people nod politely after you explain what you do.

    You are a copywriter who writes plain, specific positioning for service businesses. My ideal client: [paste the 6 bullets from prompt 1] How I describe what I do today: [paste] A real result I've delivered: [example with a number, if you have one] 1. Write 5 versions of the sentence "I help [who] [get result] without [pain they hate]," from most conservative to boldest. 2. For each, rate from 1 to 10 how clearly a busy stranger would understand it in 5 seconds. 3. Write a 3-sentence version I can say out loud when someone asks "So what do you do?", ending with a question that invites them to tell me about their situation. 4. Suggest one simple starter offer (fixed scope, fixed price, done in 2 to 4 weeks) that's an easy yes for this client.

    Then: Put the winning sentence in your LinkedIn headline, email signature and profile bios this week.

  3. 03

    Build a list of 30 people worth contacting

    Find

    Use it when you have nobody to reach out to this week.

    You are a research assistant building a prospect list for a [your role]. My ideal client: [paste] Where I'm based and who I can serve: [location or "remote, worldwide"] Part A. List 10 specific places these people gather: named LinkedIn groups, Slack or Facebook communities, associations, newsletters, podcasts, events and hashtags. For each, add one sentence on how to show up there helpfully, without pitching. Part B. Give me 5 search strings I can paste into LinkedIn or Google to find people who match (job titles, keywords, company size signals). Part C. List 5 "buying signals" that tell me someone needs help right now (for example: just hired, just launched, posting about a problem, job ad for a role I could cover). Part D. Give me a simple scoring rule from 1 to 3 so I contact the best 30 first.

    Then: Spend 30 minutes using the search strings and fill a list of 30 names, starting with anyone you already know.

Reach

  1. 04

    Teach AI to write like you

    Reach

    Do this once. It makes every message after it sound like you, not like a robot.

    Here are 3 things I've written that sound like me (an email, a post and a text are ideal): 1. [paste] 2. [paste] 3. [paste] Study them and write my "voice rules": 6 to 8 short rules covering tone, sentence length, words and phrases I use, words I never use, how I open and close messages, and how formal I am. Then rewrite this generic message using my rules: "Hi, I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out to see if you might be interested in my services." From now on in this conversation, follow my voice rules in everything you write for me. No exclamation marks unless I use them. Never write "I hope this finds you well," "just circling back" or "touch base." Keep messages shorter than you think they should be.

    Then: Save your voice rules in your AI tool's custom instructions or project instructions so you don't have to repeat this.

  2. 05

    Write a first message that gets a reply

    Reach

    Use it for every new prospect. One great message beats ten generic ones.

    You are helping me start a real conversation with a prospect, not pitch them. Who they are: [name, role, company] Something specific I found: [paste a recent post, a line from their website, news, a job ad] What I help with: [your sentence from prompt 2] Write 3 versions of a first message (LinkedIn or email), each under 80 words: - Version A opens with the specific detail and asks a question about it. - Version B opens with a short observation about a problem people in their role often have, then asks if it's true for them. - Version C offers one small, free idea that's useful whether or not they ever hire me. Rules: mention one specific detail, no flattery, no links, no "I'd love to hop on a call." End with an easy question they can answer in one line. Follow my voice rules. Then tell me which version you'd send and why.

    Then: Send it, log it in your tracker with today's date, and set a follow-up for 4 days from now.

  3. 06

    Turn your warm network into referrals

    Reach

    Use it before any cold outreach. People who already know you are far more likely to reply than strangers.

    Help me reconnect with people who already know and trust me. Person: [name], [how I know them], last talked [when] Something real I remember about them or our work: [detail] What I'm focused on now: [your sentence from prompt 2] Write: 1. A warm check-in under 70 words that's about them first, mentions what I'm doing now in one line, and asks nothing yet. 2. A follow-up for after they reply, asking a specific referral question: "Who do you know who [specific situation]?" (Specific situations get referrals; "let me know if you hear of anyone" doesn't.) 3. A 3-sentence blurb about me they can forward without editing. 4. A short thank-you for when they make an introduction, plus the first message I'd send to the person they introduce.

    Then: Run this for 10 people this week. Start with the past clients who'd say the nicest things about you.

  4. 07

    Follow up without being annoying

    Reach

    Use it when someone hasn't replied. Plenty of replies come on the second or third touch.

    I sent this message [number] days ago and got no reply: [paste the message] About them: [role, company, anything you know] Write a 4-touch follow-up sequence over 3 weeks. Each touch must add something new and useful, never just "bumping this up": - Touch 1 (day 4): a quick, relevant tip or observation about their business. - Touch 2 (day 9): a short story or result from a similar client, in 2 sentences. - Touch 3 (day 15): a free resource or idea they can use right away. - Touch 4 (day 21): a polite close-the-loop message that makes "not now" easy to say. Each under 60 words. Different opening line each time. Follow my voice rules.

    Then: Put the four dates in your tracker now so you don't have to remember them.

Qualify

  1. 08

    Run a 15-minute fit call

    Qualify

    Use it when someone says yes to a chat. Not everyone who says yes is a buyer; this call tells you who is.

    You are a sales coach. I have a 15-minute fit call with [name], [role] at [company]. What I know so far: [paste their message, website summary, or form answers] My offer: [your offer and its price range] A good client passes four checks: 1. Fit: we'd work well together and they value what I do. 2. Realistic expectations: they see this as a process, not instant magic. 3. A problem I can solve: it's specific and it's costing them something. 4. Ready to act: they can make the decision, it matters now (7 or more out of 10), and they expect to invest. Write me a minute-by-minute plan for the call: - A 2-sentence opening that sets the purpose: "see if there's a fit and whether a longer conversation makes sense." - 2 questions for each of the four checks, worded conversationally, plus one follow-up for vague answers. - The green flags and red flags to listen for under each check. - Two closing scripts: one that books a longer discovery call on the spot, one that kindly exits and offers a free resource instead.

    Then: Aim to listen 70% of the time. Write down their exact words about the problem; you'll use them in prompts 10, 13 and 14.

  2. 09

    Create a pre-call questionnaire

    Qualify

    Use it to screen leads before you give them an hour of your time.

    Write a short form a prospect fills in before booking a call with me. I'm a [your role] and my main offer is [offer, typical price range]. Requirements: - 6 to 8 questions, under 5 minutes to complete. - Cover: what they want to fix, what it's costing them, what they've tried, timeline, who decides, and budget range (with multiple-choice ranges, not an open box). - One question that tells me how motivated they are. - A friendly 2-sentence intro at the top explaining why I ask. Then tell me, for each question, which answers mean "book a call," "send a resource instead" or "not a fit."

    Then: Put the form in Google Forms, Tally or Typeform and make it the step before your booking link.

Discover and close

  1. 10

    Plan a discovery call that ends in a decision

    Close

    Use it before every serious sales call.

    You are a consultative sales coach. Help me plan a 45-minute discovery call with [name], [role] at [company]. Notes from our fit call or their form: [paste, including their exact words] My offer: [offer], [price or range] Build the call in 7 parts, with timings, and give me 2 to 3 sayable lines or questions for each: 1. Reconnect (3 min): pick up where we left off, using something they told me. 2. Set the agenda (2 min): what we'll cover, and a promise that they'll leave with more clarity whether or not we work together. 3. Current situation (12 min): their 3 biggest problems. For each one: what's causing it, how long it's been going on, what they've tried, and what it costs them each month in money, time and stress. 4. Where they want to be (8 min): "Imagine it's 12 months from now and this went really well. What changed?" Then pin down the number they'd use to measure success. 5. Play it back (5 min): a summary of their problems, the cost and their goal, in their own words, ending with "Did I get that right?" 6. Recommend a path (10 min): how I'd help, step by step, starting with the smallest sensible first step, with the investment. 7. Next step (5 min): ask for the decision. If they need time, book a short decision call within 2 to 3 days before we hang up. Also list the 3 objections I'm most likely to hear from this person and one calm response to each.

    Then: Don't pitch until part 6. The more they talk in parts 3 and 4, the easier part 6 gets.

  2. 11

    Put a price tag on the problem

    Close

    Use it right after the discovery call, so your price looks small next to what the problem costs them.

    Here are my notes from a discovery call: [paste] Act as a financial analyst. Estimate what this client's problem costs them: 1. List each cost you can find in my notes: hours lost (times a realistic hourly cost), revenue they're missing, leads or customers lost, mistakes and rework, and costs they mentioned directly. 2. Show the math for each as a monthly and yearly figure. Use their numbers where they gave them; where you're estimating, say so and keep it conservative. 3. Compare the total to my price of [price]: how many weeks of the problem does my fee equal? 4. Write 2 sentences I can use in my proposal that connect the cost to the investment, without exaggerating. If you'd need a number I didn't capture, list the question I should ask them.

    Then: Put the monthly cost of the problem on the first page of your proposal, in their words.

  3. 12

    Rehearse the objections before you hear them

    Close

    Use it before an important call, or after one where you froze.

    Play the role of [describe the prospect: role, business, personality], who is interested in my [offer] at [price] but hesitant. Their real concern is [budget / timing / trust / partner needs to agree / "we'll do it ourselves"], but don't tell me that directly. We'll role-play the last 10 minutes of a sales call. Raise objections the way a real person would: vague at first ("I need to think about it"), and only revealing the real concern if I ask good questions. After 8 to 10 exchanges, step out of the role and give me feedback: - Did I find the real objection, or answer the surface one? - Where did I talk too much or get defensive? - Better wording for my 2 weakest answers. - A score from 1 to 10. Start the role-play now with: "This all sounds good, I just need to think about it."

    Then: Practice the ones you score lowest on. The useful lines to remember: "What specifically would you want to think about?" and "If we solved that, would you want to move forward?"

  4. 13

    Write a proposal with three options

    Close

    Use it the same day as the call, while it's fresh for both of you.

    Write a one-page proposal from my call notes: [paste] The cost of the problem (from prompt 11): [paste] What I'm offering: [scope], [timeline], [price] Structure: 1. Where you are now: their situation and problems, in their own words. 2. Where you want to be: their goal and the number they'll measure. 3. What it's costing you: one line with the monthly cost. 4. Three options: Essential (the smallest first step), Recommended (the one I'd choose) and Complete, each with what's included, timeline and price. Make Recommended the clear best value. 5. Why me: 2 sentences, with one relevant result. 6. Next step: exactly how to say yes (reply, sign or pay), and the start date. Plain language, no jargon, under 450 words. Follow my voice rules.

    Then: Send it within 24 hours, and walk them through it on the decision call instead of just emailing it into silence.

Follow through

  1. 14

    Follow up after the call until you get a clear answer

    Follow through

    Use it when a proposal is out and they've gone quiet.

    I sent a proposal to [name] on [date] for [offer] at [price]. From our call, they want [their goal] and the problem is costing them about [cost]. Their main hesitation was: [concern, or "unknown"] Write: 1. A recap email for today: their problem, goal and the cost of waiting, in their words, plus the decision call time (or two times to choose from). Under 120 words. 2. A day-3 follow-up that answers their main hesitation with one useful fact, example or smaller option. 3. A day-7 message that asks for a clear yes, no or "not now," and makes all three easy to say. 4. A kind "not now" reply that agrees on a date to check back and keeps the door open. Follow my voice rules. No guilt, no fake urgency.

    Then: A clear "no" is better than months of maybe. Close the loop and move the deal to won, lost or check back in your tracker.

  2. 15

    Run your weekly pipeline hour

    Follow through

    Use it every Monday. A pipeline that's tended every week never runs dry.

    You are my sales assistant. I can spend [minutes] a week on getting clients. My goal is [number] new clients by [date] at about [average price]. Here's my pipeline (paste from your tracker): [name, stage, last contact, next step] 1. Work backwards from my goal: how many conversations, calls and proposals I need each week (assume a 20% to 30% close rate on proposals, and say so). 2. Give me this week's plan in order: who to follow up with today, how many new people to reach, and one visibility action (a post, a comment, an intro). 3. Flag any deal that's been stuck more than 14 days and suggest the next step for each. 4. Give me a simple tracker layout (columns) if mine is missing something. 5. End with the single most important thing to do in the next 30 minutes.

    Then: Put a recurring 60-minute block on your calendar called "Pipeline." Treat it like a client meeting.
